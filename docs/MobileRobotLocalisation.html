Mobile Robot Localisation

Navigation is one of, if NOT, the most challenging problem faced by an AMRAutonomous Mobile RoboticsAutonomous Mobile Robotics. This is due to numerous factors such as a dynamically changing environments or the uncertainty from measurements which gives an imperfect image of the environment. Under these limitations, for the robot to be able to successfully navigate its environment, it requires four (4) functions:

Perception

the robot must be able to interpret its sensors to extract meaningful data,

Localisation

the robot must be able to determine its position within the environment,

Cognition

the robot must be able to decide how to act to achieve its goals,

Motion control

the robot must be able to modulate its motor outputs to achieve the desired trajectory.

Of these four (4) aforementioned components, localisation has received the greatest attention for research and development in the past and, as a result, significant advances have been made on this front, which are documented in [†]Bailey, T. Mobile robot localisation and mapping in extensive outdoor environments PhD thesis (Citeseer, 2002), [†]Campbell, S. et al. Where am I? Localization techniques for mobile robots a review in 2020 6th International Conference on Mechatronics and Robotics Engineering (ICMRE) (2020), 43–47, and [†]Hodge, V. J. Sensors and data in mobile robotics for localisation. Encyclopedia of Data Science and Machine Learning, 2223–2238 (2023). In this chapter, we will explore the successful localisation methodologies and techniques used in academic research and industrial application [†]Jaradat, O. et al. Challenges of safety assurance for industry 4.0 in 2017 13th European Dependable Computing Conference (EDCC) (2017), 103–106.

As this chapter is fairly comprehensive a detailed description of it content is presented below:

  • We will start by describing how sensor and effector uncertainty is responsible for the difficulties of localisation in Section 1.1,

  • Then, in Section 1.2, we will have a look at the two (2) extreme approaches to dealing with the challenge of robot localisation [†]Filliat, D. & Meyer, J.-A. Map-based navigation in mobile robots:: I. a review of localization strategies. Cognitive systems research 4, 243–282 (2003):

    • Avoiding localisation altogether, and

    • Performing explicit map-based localisation.

  • The remainder of the chapter discusses the question of representation, which we will have a look at different case studies of successful localisation systems using a variety of representations and techniques to achieve AMRAutonomous Mobile RoboticsAutonomous Mobile Robotics localisation.
